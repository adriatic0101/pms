California county research project

Can place help explain PMS symptom severity?

This project is designed to test whether social determinants and lifestyle context are associated with validated PMS outcomes among reproductive-aged women. The design is ready; the required county-level outcome data are not.

Data landscape

The difference between an available correlate and a valid outcome is the central constraint of this study.

Available county context

ACS supports income, poverty, and educational attainment. CDC PLACES supports food insecurity, short sleep, physical inactivity, and lack of health insurance.

Unavailable dependent variable

No audited public source provides validated PMS severity or prevalence for reproductive-aged women across every California county.

Acceptable measurement

A documented PSST or DRSP measure with county FIPS, eligible denominators, survey years, population definition, provenance, and uncertainty.

Unacceptable substitutes

Menstrual irregularity, depression, pelvic pain, search activity, general distress, or synthetic PMS scores cannot answer the stated research question.

Analysis workflow

The pipeline stops at step two unless the outcome passes every provenance and coverage check.

Acquire exposures

Download real ACS and CDC PLACES county measures.

Validate PMS outcome

Require one real, validated measure across 58 counties.

Estimate associations

Run correlations and prespecified robust county models.

Report cautiously

Map patterns with uncertainty and ecological limitations.

Interpretation boundary: Even with a qualifying outcome, this would be a 58-county ecological study. It could describe county-level associations, not individual risk or causal effects. Contextual measures for all adults may also differ from the experience of reproductive-aged women.
